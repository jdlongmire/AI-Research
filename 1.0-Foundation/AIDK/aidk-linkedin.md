# LinkedIn Post: AIDK Framework

---

Your AI answers every question with the same confident tone—whether it's correct or hallucinating a Supreme Court case that never existed.

That's not a bug. It's architectural.

I've been developing a framework to understand this: the AI Dunning-Kruger Effect (AIDK).

The human Dunning-Kruger effect is correctable. We bump into reality, fail, and recalibrate. AI systems can't do this. They operate in a closed symbolic space with no grounding wire to what actually is.

The real problem? When AI's structural overconfidence meets human uncertainty, something amplifies.

I call it the Interactive Dunning-Kruger Effect (IDKE):
- You ask about something outside your expertise
- AI responds with confident fluency
- You can't evaluate correctness (that's why you asked)
- AI can't signal its own unreliability
- Your confidence increases without warrant

The AI's inability to know what it doesn't know gets laundered into your confident assertion.

The people most vulnerable to the human version are most amplified by the AI version.

The framework proposes HCAE—stratifying AI deployment by the epistemic authority of the human in the loop. "Human in the loop" is not undifferentiated.

Full writeup with the formal framework (now on Zenodo): https://airesearchandphilosophy.substack.com/p/the-ai-dunning-kruger-effect-why

The system will never know what it doesn't know. Design accordingly.
